centripetal / centrifugal
/ˌsɛnˈtrɪpɪtəl/ · /ˌsɛnˈtrɪfjʊɡəl/ — Latin, centrum + petere / fugere
To seek the center, or to flee it.
Two forces. One pulls inward, creating gravity wells.
The other moves outward, toward connection.
I

The gravity well

Centripetal — from Latin centrum + petere, to seek the center. A force that pulls inward. Creates gravity wells. Concentrates everything toward a single point.

In the context of the attention economy, centripetal is the dominant force. It's not about expanding, exploring, becoming better or more interesting or growing. It's just about keeping more of you. They don't want you to go off and create something beautiful on your own. They want you to stay, to scroll, to consume.

The centripetal force doesn't want you to leave.
It wants to keep drip-feeding you
so you don't actually go create anything on your own.

Caught in the gratification loop, reaving externalized consequences. Time dissolves, and you've lived through myriad dreams without touching reality. You browse twenty recipes and you're still hungry. The signifier, more valuable than the signified. The salon — where ideas collided, where strangers became collaborators — replaced by an infinite feed optimized for time-on-platform.

II

Escape velocity

Centrifugal — from Latin centrum + fugere, to flee the center. A force that moves outward. Decentralized, distributed. Not retention but release. Not a gravity well but a launchpad.

The opposite of a retention loop is an invitation to participate — not a notification, not a nudge, not a dark pattern, but escape velocity. An encouragement not just to connect and create but to go — to explore, to uncover new beauty in the world, to pour more love and joy into it. To grow and tend beautiful things.

Not a gravity well but a launchpad.
Not retention but release.
An encouragement to actually make something.

Imagine a system that is centrifugal rather than centripetal — one that moves outward, decentralized, rather than pulling everything into a gravity well. Where the trail you leave is legible, and yours. The difference between surveillance and legibility is directional: one extracts value from you, the other reveals value to you.

III

The word we're still searching for

Centrifugal captures one aspect — the outward push, the decentralized quality. But it doesn't sound that nice. It's not the refined kind of word. It captures physics but not philosophy. It describes the direction but not the why.

There's something in the neighborhood that captures the outward, distributed, life-at-the-edges quality more precisely. Maybe it's something in the realm of ecotone — because that's just such a lovely, amazing concept. The tension between two homes as the generative space. The boundary as the place where the most interesting things happen.

Centrifugal is one. It doesn't sound that nice,
but it captures one aspect.
The search continues.

What we're reaching toward: a word that names the entire philosophy. Not just outward force, but the principle that life is richest at edges, that crown shyness is more beautiful than dominance, that the invitation to create is more powerful than the compulsion to consume. The word exists somewhere. We haven't found it yet.

IV

Directionality

Everything is directional. The centripetal force creates bounded containers — algorithms that keep you inside, platforms that monetize your attention, systems designed to make you stay. The centrifugal force creates doorways — tools that launch you outward, invitations that embrace with the soul, systems that make you more capable of going off and building something extraordinary.

The sillage you leave is shaped by which force you're moving under. Consumed under centripetal gravity, your trail is harvested, flattened, commodified. Moving under centrifugal intention, your trail becomes legible — a living record of curiosity, creation, and care.

Build launchpads, not gravity wells.
Create doorways, not walls.
Invitations, not obligations.

The word we're looking for
lives somewhere in the neighborhood of ecotone.
We'll know it when we find it.
